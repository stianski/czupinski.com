01

How this test works

This is a decision tree, not a language model. The same set of answers always gives the same result, and every path can be checked against the Act. The test draws attention to four borderline cases.

Secondary activity

The Annexes list types of entities, not a company's main line of business, so secondary activities count as well. For drinking water and waste water, the Act excludes entities for which this is a non-essential part of their activity.

ICT services within a group

Managed service providers are listed in Annex 1, so ICT services provided to other entities, including within a corporate group, may mean that the provisions apply.

Linked enterprises

Size is calculated together with the figures of linked and partner enterprises. The exception: when you exceed the thresholds only because of that aggregation and your information system is independent of theirs, or you do not provide services jointly with them.

Supply chain

A company outside the scope of the Act may receive security questionnaires and contractual requirements from customers who are subject to it.

02

Questions

Can a small company fall under the KSC Act?
Micro and small enterprises are, as a rule, not subject to it. The exceptions are activities covered by the Act regardless of company size, for example DNS services, domain name registration and qualified trust services. Also check whether you exceed the thresholds once linked enterprises are included.
What is the difference between an essential and an important entity?
An essential entity has the full set of statutory obligations and a mandatory security audit at least once every 3 years. The obligations of an important entity are similar, without the mandatory periodic audit.
By when do the obligations have to be implemented?
Entities that met the criteria on 3 April 2026 must implement the obligations (including the information security management system and incident handling) by 3 April 2027. Others have 12 months from meeting the criteria.
The Act does not apply to me. Does it still affect me?
Your customers may be subject to the Act. They have to look after the security of their supply chain, so you may receive security questionnaires and contractual requirements even if you are not subject to the provisions yourself.
